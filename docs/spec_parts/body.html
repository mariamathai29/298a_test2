1. Operating specification

The design was built against a 50 MHz target, where the slow corner misses by 1.9 ns, and is specified at 40 MHz, where every corner passes with margin. Building at a lower target doesn't help: the resizer relaxes and the result is worse (builds at 44 and 46 MHz came out at −1.7 and −2.2 ns). So the flow keeps the 50 MHz place-and-route constraint and signs off at the spec.

2. Function and number formats

A 4-tap FIR filter whose weights adapt by sign-sign LMS. The reference u feeds the FIR; r is the desired input. The chip returns y (the filter output) and e = r − y (the error). Two uses share the same datapath:

  • Interference cancellation: r = signal + interference correlated with u; e is the cleaned signal.
  • System identification: r is an unknown system's response to u; the weights converge to that system's impulse response and are read out over the debug path.

Per sample n, for k = 0..3:

y[n] = sat8( round( Σ x[n−k] · w[k] ) )    e[n] = sat8( r[n] − y[n] )    w[k] ← sat14( w[k] + μ · sign(e[n]) · sign(x[n−k]) )

sign(0) = 0; sign(e) comes from the exact 9-bit r − y, before saturation. The update uses the same x vector that produced y[n]. μ = 2mu_sel weight-register LSBs = 2mu_sel − 12. The FIR multiplies by the top 10 bits of each weight.

4. Interfaces and host protocol

Input port: SPI slave, mode 0

The host sends u and r together on two lanes, MSB first, with CS_IN low. It changes data on the falling SCLK_IN edge and the chip samples on the rising edge. After the 8th rising edge both words move into holding registers and one filter step starts. CS_IN must then go high (or SCLK_IN stop) long enough that consecutive samples are at least 23 clk apart. The spec uses 13 SCLK_IN periods per sample: 8 data bits plus 5 with CS_IN high.

Output port: frame-sync serial master

Each step produces one 8-bit frame carrying y and e on two lanes, MSB first. The chip drives SCLK_OUT = clk ÷ 2. FS_OUT is high for the first bit. Data changes on the falling SCLK_OUT edge, and the receiver samples on the rising edge, a full clk period (25 ns) later. Between frames SCLK_OUT and FS_OUT idle low. A frame takes 16 clk, so it always finishes before the next step's frame (≥ 23 clk).

Host procedure

  1. Reset: hold rst_n low for at least 4 clk with CS_IN high, then release it. The SCLK_IN-domain counter is cleared by rst_n or CS_IN, so CS_IN must be high when rst_n rises.
  2. Configure: set adapt_en and mu_sel. They pass through 2-flop synchronisers. Change mu_sel between samples, or while adapt_en = 0, so that one sample can't see a mix of old and new bits.
  3. Stream: send frames at ≥ 23 clk spacing (spec: 26). Each one returns a y/e frame about 0.9 µs after its last bit.
  4. Read the weights: set adapt_en = 0, wait for the last y/e frame, then raise debug_i1. Four frames follow (weight k on the y lane, tap k on the e lane, k = 0..3), 20 clk apart. Samples sent during the 80 clk readout are dropped and set debug_o1. Set adapt_en = 1 again to resume.
  5. Flags: debug_o1 (overrun) and debug_o2 (saturation) are sticky until reset.

5. Architecture

Rotating rings instead of a read mux

The taps and weights are circular shift registers. Each MAC and UPD cycle uses position 0 and rotates both rings by one, so every register bit connects only to its neighbour, and after 4 rotations the rings are back in order. The earlier v3 had one MAC/update unit reaching every tap and weight through a 4:1 mux and a write decoder. That needed 168% of Metal3, the tile's only horizontal routing layer, and never routed. The rings brought Metal3 down to 45%.

Horner-order radix-4 Booth multiply

Each 8-bit tap splits into four radix-4 Booth digits d3..d0 ∈ {−2, −1, 0, +1, +2}, so x = Σ 4jdj. The MAC runs digit outer, tap inner:

acc = ((S3·4 + S2)·4 + S1)·4 + S0,   where Sj = Σk dj(x[k]) · w[k][13:4]

So acc only ever shifts by a fixed 2 bits (just wiring) at the end of a digit group, and the tap order inside a group is exactly the ring rotation. The accumulator starts at 2; Horner scales it by 43 to 128 = 27, which is the round-half-up constant for the final ≫ 8. No separate rounding adder is needed.

7. Clock-domain crossing (no FIFO)

  1. On every rising SCLK_IN edge, the u and r lanes shift into 7-bit registers. A 3-bit counter, cleared asynchronously while CS_IN is high, marks the 8th bit.
  2. On the 8th bit, the full words go into the 8-bit holding registers and d_tog flips.
  3. In the clk domain, d_s[2:0] samples the toggle. d_pre = d_s[1] ⊕ d_s[0] decides the step start one cycle early, and d_stb = d_s[2] ⊕ d_s[1] drives the overrun flag.
  4. The registered step-start copies (go) copy hold u into the tap ring and hold r into r_q.

Data stability: the holding registers change only at the next frame's 8th bit, ≥ 23 clk later. The core reads them 2–3 clk after the toggle, so the words are stable when sampled and only the single toggle bit really crosses. The two clocks are declared asynchronous in STA, which is correct because of this protocol.

Metastability: d_pre uses the first synchroniser stage d_s[0], so the protection comes from slack, not a second flop. STA on the routed netlist gives the paths from d_s[0] to the four go flops @@CDCSS@@ ns of slack at the slow corner (@@CDCTT@@ ns typical) at 40 MHz. That's the time a metastable d_s[0] has to resolve. MTBF = et/τ / (Tw · fclk · fdata); with Tw = 0.1 ns, 40 MHz, 1.54 MS/s and an assumed slow-corner flop time constant τ = 0.3 ns, that's about @@MTBF@@ years. It stays above 1,000 years for any τ up to @@TAU1K@@ ns. The GF180 library doesn't publish τ, so a later revision could take d_pre from d_s[2:1] and add a stage, at the cost of one clk of latency and none of throughput.

Control pins (adapt_en, mu_sel, debug_i1) go through independent 2-flop synchronisers. They're static or slow, so they're false-pathed at the pins. The readout trigger is edge-detected after synchronisation.

8. Timing closure and signoff

What Tiny Tapeout requires

Tiny Tapeout's precheck has no timing check. It runs DRC (Magic and KLayout), pin, boundary, power-pin, layer, cell-name, antenna, analog-pin and Verilog checks only. Their tools print clock warnings from the typical corner (nom_tt_025C_3v30) alone. The published I/O figures are for Sky130 (clock up to about 66 MHz, GPIO output 33 MHz, input 66 MHz, about 20 ns round trip, up to 10 ns clock insertion delay); there are no GF180 timing or corner criteria. This design applies a stricter, self-imposed signoff: setup and hold at all 9 LibreLane corners, with the I/O model below.

Why the absolute delays don't matter here: every interface is either source-synchronous (SPI in: SCLK_IN travels with its data; SPI out: SCLK_OUT travels with its data) or synchronised (control pins). There's no path from a pad through the mux and into a clk-launched or clk-captured flop with a board-level timing relationship. So the 3–12 ns mux delay and the 4–15 ns clock insertion cancel, and what's left is the mismatch between pins. The constraints (src/af_timing.sdc) model exactly that:

  • SPI in: set_input_delay ±(2 + @@IOSKEW@@) ns on CS_IN and both MOSI lanes, relative to the falling SCLK_IN edge.
  • SPI out: a generated clock sclk_out (clk ÷ 2) at the uo_out[0] port, so STA measures the tile's own skew between SCLK_OUT and data exactly. Output delays are +(tsu + skew) and −(th + skew). A hold multicycle reflects that data changes on falling SCLK_OUT, a full clk after the sampling edge.
  • Async pins (control, reset, flags) are false paths.
  • PnR uses pnr.sdc (clk 20 ns, SCLK_IN 40 ns) so the resizer keeps pushing; signoff uses signoff.sdc (clk 25 ns, SCLK_IN 50 ns).

10. Verification

Method. test/af_cases.py builds each stimulus and predicts every output frame (y, e, weight and tap readouts, flags) with lms_model.py (4 taps, μ = 2mu_sel LSBs). The cocotb testbench test/test.py (wrapper test/tb.v, Icarus Verilog) acts as SPI master and frame-sync receiver, and every captured frame is compared bit for bit. The same cases run on the RTL, the routed netlist with zero delay, and the routed netlist back-annotated with the signoff SDF at the slow and fast corners. test/dv/run_all.sh runs the full matrix.

CaseStimulus and checksRTLGLSDF ssSDF ff

Clocks at the spec unless noted: clk 40 MHz, SCLK_IN 20 MHz. "stress" re-runs the old 50 MHz / 25 MHz target functionally (RTL and zero-delay GL only). Icarus applies SDF delays but does not evaluate $setup/$hold checks; STA covers those. 2,890 of 2,900 cells are annotated; the 10 replicated ctl_ff flops have escaped hierarchical names Icarus's SDF reader can't match, and keep the model's default delays.

Does it adapt? System identification through the chip's own readout

The sysid case drives white noise u (σ = 0.35) through an unknown plant h = [+0.4375, −0.28125, +0.15625, +0.0625] plus noise (σ = 0.004) to form r. It adapts at μ = 2−7 for 1,500 samples, then 2−10 for 2,500. Every 100 samples it freezes adaptation, reads the weights out of the chip, and resumes. Pass criteria: final readout within 3 LSB (0.047) of h for every tap, and error power at least 20 dB below r over the last 1,000 samples. Result: max |w − h| = 1 LSB (0.016); error 30.9 dB below r. The trace is identical in RTL, GL and both SDF corners.

11. Limitations and open items

  1. TT GF180 mux delays are estimated from Sky130 measurements. Because every interface is source-synchronous or synchronised, only pin-to-pin mismatch matters, and the margin (≥ 10 ns) absorbs a large error.
  2. Utilisation is @@UTIL@@. There's little room for added logic. 12-bit weights (Q2.10) would save about 1,000 µm² at the cost of μ ≥ 2−10.
  3. d_pre uses the first synchroniser stage (see section 7). It's safe on slack, and hardening it would cost one clk of latency.
  4. mu_sel is a 3-bit bus through independent synchronisers. Change it between samples or while frozen.
  5. Readout freezes adaptation and costs 80 clk, during which samples are dropped.
  6. Exactly 2 MS/s is not supported at 40 MHz (the maximum is 1.74 MS/s). The original 2 MS/s target needs clk ≥ 46 MHz, which the slow corner doesn't support.
  7. Slew at the slow corner: @@SLEWSHORT@@
